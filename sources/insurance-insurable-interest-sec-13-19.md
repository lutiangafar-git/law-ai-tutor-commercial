# Source text
Section 13. Every interest in property, whether real or personal, or any relation thereto, or liability in respect thereof, of such nature that a contemplated peril might directly damnify the insured, is an insurable interest.

Section 17. The measure of an insurable interest in property is the extent to which the insured might be damnified by loss or injury thereof.

Section 18. No contract or policy of insurance on property shall be enforceable except for the benefit of some person having an insurable interest in the property insured.

Section 19. An interest in property insured must exist when the insurance takes effect, and when the loss occurs, but need not exist in the meantime; and interest in the life or health of a person insured must exist when the insurance takes effect, but need not exist thereafter or when the loss occurs.

# Key points for the tutor (reviewer-approved)
- Insurable interest in property is any interest, relation or liability such that the insured peril might directly damnify (cause loss to) the insured (Sec. 13).
- It is measured by how much the insured might lose from loss or injury to the property (Sec. 17).
- A property insurance policy is enforceable only for the benefit of someone with an insurable interest (Sec. 18).
- In property insurance, the interest must exist both when the insurance takes effect and when the loss occurs; it need not exist in between (Sec. 19).
- In life or health insurance, the interest need exist only when the insurance takes effect (Sec. 19).

# Recitation drill: application fact pattern
Scenario: Rina insured her own house against fire in January 2026. In June 2026 she donated and delivered the house to her son, but kept the policy. The house burned in August 2026. Can Rina collect?
Model answer: No. Under Sec. 19, an insurable interest in property must exist both when the insurance takes effect and when the loss occurs. Rina had an interest in January, but after she gave the house to her son she no longer stood to lose anything from the fire. With no insurable interest at the time of loss, the policy is not enforceable for her benefit (Sec. 18).

# Problem drill: exam or bar-style problem
Problem: (Original bar-style problem. Related issues were asked in the 2018 and 2025 Bar Examinations, Commercial Law.) In January 2026, Carlo insured his car against loss and damage for one year. In March 2026, he sold and delivered the car to Dan, who paid in full. Carlo did not tell the insurer and kept the policy. In May 2026, the car was destroyed in a fire while parked in Dan's garage. Carlo filed a claim, arguing that he had an insurable interest when the policy was issued. Should the insurer pay Carlo? Explain.
Model answer:
- Answer: No. The insurer is not liable to Carlo.
- Legal basis: Under Sec. 18 of the Insurance Code, a policy on property is enforceable only for the benefit of a person with an insurable interest in it. Under Sec. 19, an insurable interest in property must exist when the insurance takes effect and when the loss occurs, though not necessarily in between. Under Sec. 13, a person has an insurable interest when the insured peril might directly damnify him.
- Application: Carlo had an insurable interest in January, when he owned the car. But he sold and delivered it to Dan, who paid in full, in March. When the fire happened in May, the car belonged to Dan, and its loss could not damnify Carlo. Carlo's interest therefore did not exist when the loss occurred, so one of the two required moments is missing.
- Conclusion: Because Carlo had no insurable interest when the loss occurred, the policy cannot be enforced for his benefit, and his claim fails.

# Objective and multiple-choice quiz (Year 1-2)
Q (multiple choice): Under Sec. 19, an insurable interest in PROPERTY must exist:
A) only when the insurance takes effect
B) only when the loss occurs
C) when the insurance takes effect and when the loss occurs
D) at all times during the policy, without any gap
Correct: C
Hint: Sec. 19 names two moments for property insurance.
Hint: The interest "need not exist in the meantime".
Why: Sec. 19 requires the interest in property to exist when the insurance takes effect and when the loss occurs, but not in between.

Q (multiple choice): For LIFE insurance, when must the insurable interest exist under Sec. 19?
A) Only when the insurance takes effect
B) Only when the insured dies
C) Both when it takes effect and at death
D) Never; no interest is needed
Correct: A
Hint: Life insurance has a different rule from property insurance in Sec. 19.
Hint: It "need not exist thereafter or when the loss occurs".
Why: Under Sec. 19, interest in the life or health of the insured must exist when the insurance takes effect, but need not exist after that.

Q (objective): Under Sec. 13, an interest is insurable if a contemplated peril might directly ______ the insured. (One word.)
Accept: damnify | damage | harm
Hint: It means to cause loss or injury.
Why: Sec. 13 defines insurable interest by whether the peril might directly damnify the insured.

Q (objective): What is the measure of an insurable interest in property under Sec. 17: the extent to which the insured might be ______ by loss or injury to it?
Accept: damnified | damaged | harmed
Hint: It is the same word as in Sec. 13, in past form.
Why: Sec. 17 measures insurable interest by the extent the insured might be damnified by the loss.

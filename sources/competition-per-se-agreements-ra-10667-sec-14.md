# Source text
Section 14. Anti-Competitive Agreements. (a) The following agreements, between or among competitors, are per se prohibited: (1) Restricting competition as to price, or components thereof, or other terms of trade; (2) Fixing price at an auction or in any form of bidding including cover bidding, bid suppression, bid rotation and market allocation and other analogous practices of bid manipulation;

(c) Agreements other than those specified in (a) and (b) of this section which have the object or effect of substantially preventing, restricting or lessening competition shall also be prohibited: Provided, Those which contribute to improving the production or distribution of goods and services or to promoting technical or economic progress, while allowing consumers a fair share of the resulting benefits, may not necessarily be deemed a violation of this Act.

# Key points for the tutor (reviewer-approved)
- Agreements between competitors that restrict competition as to price or other terms of trade, or that fix prices in bidding (bid rigging), are per se prohibited (Sec. 14(a)).
- "Per se" means the agreement is prohibited by its nature; there is no need to prove that it actually harmed competition.
- The efficiency proviso (improving production or distribution, with a fair share for consumers) applies to the agreements in Sec. 14(c), not to the per se agreements in Sec. 14(a).
- Sec. 14 applies to agreements between or among competitors; entities under common control are not treated as competitors.

# Recitation drill: application fact pattern
Scenario: Three printing companies bidding for a government contract secretly agree that one will bid low and the other two will submit high "cover" bids, taking turns on later contracts. Is this prohibited?
Model answer: Yes. Under Sec. 14(a)(2), agreements between competitors fixing price in any form of bidding, including cover bidding and bid rotation, are per se prohibited. The three companies are competitors who agreed on cover bids and rotation, so the agreement is illegal without any need to prove its effect on competition.

# Problem drill: exam or bar-style problem
Problem: (Original bar-style problem. A related issue was asked in the 2025 Bar Examination, Commercial Law.) The three largest cement manufacturers in Mindanao, which compete with one another, agreed to sell cement at the same price of P300 per bag. Charged before the Philippine Competition Commission, they argued that the agreement prevented a ruinous price war, kept supply stable, and therefore benefited consumers. Is their defence valid? Explain.
Model answer:
- Answer: No. The defence is not valid; the price agreement is prohibited.
- Legal basis: Under Sec. 14(a)(1) of the Philippine Competition Act, agreements between or among competitors restricting competition as to price are per se prohibited. The proviso that an agreement contributing to improved production or distribution, with a fair share of benefits for consumers, may not be a violation applies only to agreements under Sec. 14(c), not to per se agreements under Sec. 14(a).
- Application: The three manufacturers are competitors, and they agreed to sell at one fixed price of P300 per bag. That is an agreement restricting competition as to price, which falls squarely under Sec. 14(a)(1). Because it is prohibited per se, the Commission need not weigh claimed benefits such as avoiding a price war or keeping supply stable.
- Conclusion: The price-fixing agreement is per se prohibited under Sec. 14(a)(1), and the claimed consumer benefits are not a defence.

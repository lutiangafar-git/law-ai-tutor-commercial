# Source text
Section 3(a) (as amended by RA 8179). The term "Philippine national" shall mean a citizen of the Philippines; or a domestic partnership or association wholly owned by citizens of the Philippines; or a corporation organized under the laws of the Philippines of which at least sixty percent (60%) of the capital stock outstanding and entitled to vote is owned and held by citizens of the Philippines; or a corporation organized abroad and registered as doing business in the Philippines under the Corporation Code of which one hundred percent (100%) of the capital stock outstanding and entitled to vote is wholly owned by Filipinos (...)

Section 5 (as amended by RA 11647). Registration of Investments of Non-Philippine Nationals. - Without need of prior approval, a non-Philippine national, as that term is defined in Section 3(a), and not otherwise disqualified by law may, upon registration with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), or the DTI in the case of single proprietorships, do business as defined in Section 3(d) of this Act or invest in a domestic enterprise up to one hundred percent (100%) of its capital, unless participation of non-Philippine nationals in the enterprise is prohibited or limited to a smaller percentage by existing law and/or under the provisions of this Act.

# Key points for the tutor (reviewer-approved)
- A domestic corporation is a Philippine national only if at least 60% of its capital stock outstanding and entitled to vote is owned and held by Filipino citizens (Sec. 3(a)).
- A corporation organized abroad and doing business here is a Philippine national only if 100% of its voting stock is owned by Filipinos (Sec. 3(a)).
- A non-Philippine national may, upon registration, invest up to 100% in a domestic enterprise, unless foreign participation is prohibited or limited by law or the Act (Sec. 5).
- Activities reserved to Philippine nationals (by the Constitution and laws, and listed in the Foreign Investment Negative List) cannot be done by an enterprise that fails the 60% test.

# Recitation drill: application fact pattern
Scenario: Bagong Ani Corp., organized in the Philippines, has 1,000 voting shares: 650 are held by Filipinos and 350 by a Singaporean investor. Is it a Philippine national under the Foreign Investments Act?
Model answer: Yes. Under Sec. 3(a), a corporation organized under Philippine law is a Philippine national if at least 60% of its capital stock outstanding and entitled to vote is owned and held by Filipino citizens. Filipinos hold 650 of 1,000 voting shares, or 65%, which is above 60%.

# Problem drill: exam or bar-style problem
Problem: (Original bar-style problem. A related issue was asked in the 2025 Bar Examination, Commercial Law.) Pinoy Media Corp. was organized under Philippine law. Of its voting shares, 55% are held by Filipino citizens and 45% by a Japanese company. It now wants to engage in an activity that existing law reserves to Philippine nationals. It argues that it is a Philippine corporation because it was organized and registered in the Philippines, and Filipinos hold the majority of its shares. Is Pinoy Media Corp. a Philippine national under the Foreign Investments Act? Explain.
Model answer:
(Answer) No. Pinoy Media Corp. is not a Philippine national under the Foreign Investments Act.

(Legal basis) Under Sec. 3(a) of RA 7042, as amended by RA 8179, a corporation organized under Philippine law is a Philippine national only if at least sixty percent (60%) of its capital stock outstanding and entitled to vote is owned and held by Filipino citizens. Activities reserved by law to Philippine nationals may not be done by an enterprise that fails this test.

(Application) Pinoy Media Corp. was organized in the Philippines, but only 55% of its voting shares are held by Filipinos. A simple Filipino majority, or being organized and registered here, is not enough; the law requires at least 60% of the voting stock. Since Filipinos hold less than 60%, the corporation is a non-Philippine national for this purpose.

(Conclusion) Pinoy Media Corp. fails the 60% Filipino-ownership requirement and so cannot engage in the activity reserved to Philippine nationals.

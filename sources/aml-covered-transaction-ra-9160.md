# Source text
Section 3(b) (as amended by RA 9194). 'Covered transaction' is a transaction in cash or other equivalent monetary instrument involving a total amount in excess of Five hundred thousand pesos (PhP 500,000.00) within one (1) banking day.

Section 9(c) (as amended by RA 10365). Covered persons shall report to the AMLC all covered transactions and suspicious transactions within five (5) working days from occurrence thereof, unless the AMLC prescribes a different period not exceeding fifteen (15) working days.

Section 9(c) (as amended by RA 9194). (...) no administrative, criminal or civil proceedings, shall lie against any person for having made a covered or suspicious transaction report in the regular performance of his duties in good faith, whether or not such reporting results in any criminal prosecution under this Act of any other law.

# Key points for the tutor (reviewer-approved)
- A covered transaction is a cash (or equivalent monetary instrument) transaction totalling more than P500,000 within one banking day (Sec. 3(b), as amended).
- The test is the total within one banking day, so splitting cash into smaller deposits on the same day does not avoid it.
- Covered persons, such as banks, must report covered and suspicious transactions to the AMLC within five working days, unless the AMLC sets a different period of up to fifteen working days (Sec. 9(c), as amended).
- Safe harbor: no administrative, criminal or civil case lies against a person who reports a covered or suspicious transaction in the regular performance of duties and in good faith (Sec. 9(c), as amended).

# Recitation drill: application fact pattern
Scenario: On one banking day, Nilo deposits P300,000 in cash in the morning and another P300,000 in cash in the afternoon at the same bank. Is this a covered transaction?
Model answer: Yes. Under Sec. 3(b), as amended, a covered transaction is a cash transaction involving a total amount above P500,000 within one banking day. Nilo's two cash deposits total P600,000 on the same banking day, so together they are a covered transaction that the bank must report.

# Problem drill: exam or bar-style problem
Problem: (Original bar-style problem. A related issue was asked in the 2025 Bar Examination, Commercial Law.) Lea, a businesswoman, deposited P800,000 in cash at Banco Dos one morning. Following the bank's procedures, a branch officer reported the deposit to the Anti-Money Laundering Council three working days later. When Lea learned of the report, she sued the officer and the bank for damages, claiming that her deposit was legitimate income and that reporting it violated her privacy. Will Lea's suit prosper? Explain.
Model answer:
- Answer: No. Lea's suit will not prosper.
- Legal basis: Under Sec. 3(b) of the Anti-Money Laundering Act, as amended, a covered transaction is a cash transaction involving a total amount in excess of P500,000 within one banking day. Covered persons must report covered transactions to the AMLC within five working days (Sec. 9(c), as amended by RA 10365). Under the safe harbor rule, no administrative, criminal or civil proceedings lie against a person who reports a covered or suspicious transaction in the regular performance of duties and in good faith (Sec. 9(c), as amended by RA 9194).
- Application: Lea's P800,000 cash deposit in one banking day exceeds P500,000, so it is a covered transaction whether or not the money is legitimate. The bank officer reported it within three working days, inside the five-day period, following the bank's procedures, that is, in the regular performance of duties and in good faith. The safe harbor therefore bars Lea's civil action.
- Conclusion: Because the report concerned a covered transaction and was made in good faith in the regular performance of duty, the safe harbor provision protects the officer and the bank, and Lea's suit should be dismissed.

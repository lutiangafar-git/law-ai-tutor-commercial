# Source text
Section 30. Liability of Directors, Trustees or Officers. - Directors or trustees who willfully and knowingly vote for or assent to patently unlawful acts of the corporation or who are guilty of gross negligence or bad faith in directing the affairs of the corporation or acquire any personal or pecuniary interest in conflict with their duty as such directors or trustees shall be liable jointly and severally for all damages resulting therefrom suffered by the corporation, its stockholders or members and other persons.

# Key points for the tutor (reviewer-approved)
- A corporation has a personality separate from its directors, so as a rule directors are not personally liable for the corporation's acts.
- Sec. 30 makes directors or trustees personally liable, jointly and severally, when they (1) willfully and knowingly vote for or assent to patently unlawful acts of the corporation, (2) are guilty of gross negligence or bad faith in directing its affairs, or (3) acquire a personal or pecuniary interest in conflict with their duty.
- The liability covers all damages suffered by the corporation, its stockholders or members, and other persons.
- An honest business decision that simply turns out badly is not, by itself, gross negligence or bad faith.

# Recitation drill: application fact pattern
Scenario: Five directors of Luzon Feeds Inc. approved, after a two-hour meeting with financial reports and a consultant's study, a plan to open a new branch. The branch lost money for a year and closed. A stockholder sues the directors personally for the losses. Are they liable under Sec. 30?
Model answer: No. As a rule, directors are not personally liable for corporate acts because the corporation has a separate personality. Sec. 30 applies only to patently unlawful acts, gross negligence or bad faith, or a conflict of interest. Here the directors studied the plan with reports and expert advice; a decision that later failed is not gross negligence or bad faith, so Sec. 30 does not apply.

# Problem drill: exam or bar-style problem
Problem: (Original bar-style problem. A related issue was asked in the 2012 Bar Examination, Commercial Law.) The board of Mabini Trading Corp. approved, without reading any documents and over the written objection of the corporate treasurer, the transfer of P20 million of corporate funds to an unregistered investment scheme promoted by the chairman's brother. Two weeks later, the scheme's operators disappeared with the money. A minority stockholder sues the directors who voted for the transfer, asking that they personally pay the corporation's loss. The directors argue that only the corporation, a separate juridical person, can be liable for corporate acts. Are the directors personally liable? Explain.
Model answer:
- Answer: Yes. The directors who voted for the transfer are personally liable, jointly and severally, for the corporation's loss.
- Legal basis: As a rule, a corporation's separate personality shields its directors from personal liability. But under Sec. 30 of the Revised Corporation Code, directors who are guilty of gross negligence or bad faith in directing the affairs of the corporation are liable jointly and severally for all damages suffered by the corporation, its stockholders or members and other persons.
- Application: The directors moved P20 million to an unregistered scheme without reading any documents and despite the treasurer's written objection. Ignoring an express warning and making no inquiry at all before risking a large part of the corporate funds is gross negligence in directing the corporation's affairs. The separate-personality defence does not protect directors who fall under Sec. 30.
- Conclusion: Because the directors acted with gross negligence, Sec. 30 makes them jointly and severally liable for the P20 million loss.

# Objective and multiple-choice quiz (Year 1-2)
Q (multiple choice): Under Sec. 30 of the Revised Corporation Code, how are directors liable when they are guilty of gross negligence or bad faith?
A) Only up to their shareholdings
B) Jointly and severally
C) Only if the corporation is insolvent
D) Only to the corporation, not to third persons
Correct: B
Hint: Sec. 30 uses three words to describe the kind of liability.
Hint: Each director can be made to answer for the whole damage.
Why: Sec. 30 makes such directors liable jointly and severally for all damages suffered by the corporation, its stockholders or members and other persons.

Q (multiple choice): Which of these is NOT a ground for a director's personal liability under Sec. 30?
A) Willfully and knowingly voting for patently unlawful acts of the corporation
B) Gross negligence or bad faith in directing the corporation's affairs
C) Acquiring a personal interest in conflict with their duty
D) Approving, in good faith and after study, a project that later loses money
Correct: D
Hint: Sec. 30 punishes unlawful, grossly negligent, bad-faith or conflicted conduct.
Hint: A careful decision that simply fails is not one of these.
Why: Sec. 30 lists unlawful acts, gross negligence or bad faith, and conflict of interest. An honest, informed decision that turns out badly is not a ground.

Q (objective): Because a corporation is a person distinct from its directors and stockholders, it is said to have a separate juridical ______.
Accept: personality | juridical personality | separate juridical personality
Hint: It is what makes the corporation, not its owners, answerable for its acts.
Why: The corporation's separate juridical personality is the general rule; Sec. 30 is an exception for erring directors.

Q (objective): Under Sec. 30, directors are liable for damages suffered by the corporation, its stockholders or members, and other ______. (One word.)
Accept: persons | people
Hint: The liability is not limited to the corporation.
Why: Sec. 30 extends liability to damages suffered by the corporation, its stockholders or members and other persons.

# Source text
Section 2. All deposits of whatever nature with banks or banking institutions in the Philippines including investments in bonds issued by the Government of the Philippines, its political subdivisions and its instrumentalities, are hereby considered as of an absolutely confidential nature and may not be examined, inquired or looked into by any person, government official, bureau or office, except upon written permission of the depositor, or in cases of impeachment, or upon order of a competent court in cases of bribery or dereliction of duty of public officials, or in cases where the money deposited or invested is the subject matter of the litigation.

Section 3. It shall be unlawful for any official or employee of a banking institution to disclose to any person other than those mentioned in Section two hereof any information concerning said deposits.

# Key points for the tutor (reviewer-approved)
- Bank deposits are absolutely confidential and may not be examined, inquired or looked into (Sec. 2).
- Exceptions under Sec. 2: (1) written permission of the depositor; (2) impeachment cases; (3) a competent court's order in cases of bribery or dereliction of duty of public officials; (4) cases where the money deposited is the subject matter of the litigation.
- Bank officials and employees may not disclose deposit information to anyone except as Sec. 2 allows (Sec. 3).
- A creditor's wish to find out whether the debtor can pay does not make the deposit the subject matter of a collection case.

# Recitation drill: application fact pattern
Scenario: During a trial for bribery, the prosecution asks the court to order a bank to disclose the deposits of a city mayor accused of taking bribes. May the court do so under RA 1405?
Model answer: Yes. Sec. 2 allows deposits to be looked into upon order of a competent court in cases of bribery or dereliction of duty of public officials. The mayor is a public official accused of bribery, so the court may order the disclosure.

# Problem drill: exam or bar-style problem
Problem: (Original bar-style problem. A related issue was asked in the 2024 Bar Examination, Commercial Law.) Lending Plus sued Rey for P2 million, the unpaid balance of a loan. Before trial, Lending Plus asked the court to order Banco Uno to disclose Rey's savings and time deposits, so it could check whether Rey has money to pay any judgment. Rey objected, invoking the secrecy of bank deposits. Should the court grant the request? Explain.
Model answer:
- Answer: No. The court should deny the request to examine Rey's deposits.
- Legal basis: Under Sec. 2 of RA 1405, all bank deposits are absolutely confidential and may not be examined, inquired or looked into, except: upon the depositor's written permission; in impeachment cases; upon a competent court's order in cases of bribery or dereliction of duty of public officials; or where the money deposited is the subject matter of the litigation.
- Application: Rey has not given written permission, and the case is neither an impeachment nor a bribery or dereliction-of-duty case against a public official. The case is a simple collection suit for an unpaid loan; its subject matter is Rey's debt, not the money in his bank accounts. Wanting to know whether Rey can pay a future judgment does not turn his deposits into the subject matter of the litigation.
- Conclusion: None of the exceptions in Sec. 2 applies, so Rey's deposits stay confidential and the court should not order their disclosure.
